Prepared 2026-10-07 for the founder, following `docs/research/market-research.md` (revised 2026-10-07), and revised the same day after a three-skeptic review (the "Skeptic review" subsection at the end of Section 9 lists each claim, verdict and what changed). Five research lanes fed it: hiring market, team-size outcomes, engine, part-time founder, agents as the team.

## 1. Answer in one page

**Does hiring change anything? It changes the date, not the outcome, and before the first wishlist gate it is not affordable in founder hours.** Across 25 co-op survival comps, team size did not set the revenue ceiling: solo, 3-person, 7-person and 55-80-person teams all produced $50M+ outcomes, while inside base-building survival every solo entrant took 4-8 years to 1.0 and landed at $1-7M lifetime ([Necesse](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/why-the-low-profile-necesse-hit-a), medium). Every comp hired from revenue, never ahead of it ([Iron Gate: three hires five months after 5M sales](https://windowscentral.com/valheims-dev-team-expand-following-games-explosive-success), medium). Each contractor costs the founder 1.5-4 h/week of 1:1s and review ([Google's reviewers average 3.2 h/week](https://research.google/pubs/pub47025), medium), 6-20% of a 15-25 h week, and engineering hires take 90 days to 9 months to reach full output ([low](https://stealthagents.com/research/time-to-productivity-new-hire-statistics-2026)). The base case is zero hires. What money buys (Section 3, revised after review): $300-1,500/month of per-asset art plus two fixed-fee architecture reviews moves quality and de-risks the design, not dates; one part-time client/feel contractor at $2-4k/month from the 90-day post-page gate pulls Early Access in by roughly 8-14 months on the median date; $8-15k/month started now buys an October 2028 Next Fest at about one chance in four and October 2029 at even odds, and February 2028 is gone at every tier below a funded studio; $25k+/month is a publisher's team.

**First hire.** None in the base case. The first *spend* is not a hire: a ~$400 main capsule ([Zukowski's figure](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-marketing-1000-budget), medium) plus $500-1,000 of capsule and art-direction iteration pulled forward into the page month, one settler rig and low-poly kits, because art is the discipline the founder and the agents both lack and the gate the whole plan hinges on is decided by capsule, hook and first screenshot (pre-fest wishlists correlate r=0.825 with Next Fest gains and streamers choose by capsule, [Zukowski via mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/forevercrab321-svg/elite-3d-design-studio/main/.claude/skills/game-sales-marketing-framework/references/research/01-zukowski-howtomarketagame.md), medium-low). The second spend is two fixed-fee senior Rust architecture reviews ($500-1,500 each) at the end of the week 0-6 spikes and at the two-player loop, where [Google's reviewer guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/eng-practices/master/review/reviewer/looking-for.md) puts review's highest value ("the most important thing to cover in a review is the overall design", high). *Revised after review:* the first *recurring* hire is no longer a fractional Rust co-reviewer. Under the founder's own merge rule, every sim, proto and persist line read by the founder, a second reviewer cannot raise the merge rate; it costs 1.5-4 h/week of a 15-25 h week; and this report's own Tier B row gave a cheaper lever a larger multiplier where the founder is weakest. The first recurring contractor, decided at the 90-day post-page gate (Aug-Sep 2028 at a demonstrated 20 h/week, Apr-May 2029 at the 15 h/week base), is a client/gameplay-and-feel engineer or technical artist in the chosen engine's C# pool at 10-15 h/week ($1,400-4,800/month) on 3-month milestone contracts, reviewed by evidence so it adds no line-by-line load. A part-time community manager at $1,000-2,000/month ([Work With Indies](https://workwithindies.com/careers/omee-life-social-media-community-manager), medium) if wishlists bind. A fractional senior Rust co-reviewer only on a review-saturation trigger, decoupled from wishlists (Section 7). The standing rule: never a second output stream the founder must read line by line before revenue.

**Engine and server.** Keep Rust for the authoritative server and the shared `sim`/`proto`/`net` crates as a plain tokio binary over Steam Datagram Relay via steamworks-rs; this half survived review untouched. *Revised after review:* the client is written in C# on an engine-agnostic .NET Standard 2.1 library (protocol, Steam, interpolation buffers, typed events) that compiles unchanged into Godot 4.7 C# or Unity 6, and the engine is chosen by a weeks 0-6 bake-off that builds the identical vertical slice in both and picks on logged founder hours, with Unity 6 as the pre-registered tie rule. The Rust GDExtension (gdext) is off the critical path: under the sidecar default it bought a one-point matrix edge while adding a one-maintainer dependency that shipped two breaking minors in six months (0.4.0 on 2025-09-29, 0.5.0 on 2026-03-27, [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/godot), verified), the cadence this report used to disqualify Bevy. The wire format moves from postcard to a schema-driven cross-language format so the Rust proto crate stays the single source of truth for a C# client. Re-scored on the founder's three criteria, Unity 6 leads Godot C# 62 to 58 of 75, inside the matrix's noise (Section 5). Bevy is dropped: 0.20 is still rc.2 today ([crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/bevy), verified), 2-3 breaking releases land per year, and its hiring pool is one-sixth of Unity's by Discord size ([23,359 vs 135,631](https://bevy.org/news/bevys-sixth-birthday/), medium).

**Part-time plan.** *Revised after review:* this report's first draft computed every date at 20 h/week for 42 straight months while its own evidence said 10-15 is the likely pace after month six, assumed 48 working weeks, budgeted no hours for devlogs, the Thursday group or engine upgrades, and printed a single "likely" point with no band. Re-baselined at 42 weeks, 2-3 h/week of community work and 8% for upgrades, the founder has ~385 build-hours a year at 15 h/week and ~540 at 20, not ~770. The base case is now 15 h/week on the pre-decided cut scope (Longthaw single season: one kit, one region, 2-player listen server only), with 20 h/week treated as an upside the founder must demonstrate (18+ h/week logged for six consecutive months) before scope is added back. On that base the two-player loop lands Aug-Sep 2028, the Steam page Jan-Feb 2029, the 90-day gate Apr-May 2029, a public demo Nov-Dec 2030 (P20) or mid-2032 (P50), and Early Access mid-2031 (P20), mid-2033 (P50) or late 2035 (P90). At a demonstrated 20 h/week: loop Feb-Mar 2028, page May-Jun 2028, gate Aug-Sep 2028, demo Sep-Oct 2029 (P20) or late 2030 (P50), EA Mar-Apr 2030 (P20), mid-2031 (P50), 2033 (P90). February 2028 is unreachable at any part-time band with zero hires, and the first draft's October 2029 fest moves from headline to stretch. The honest reading: the part-time phase reliably produces a two-player loop and a Steam page by 2028-29 and the evidence for an employment decision at the gate; at any sustainable pace it does not produce Early Access before 2030.

**Does the concept change?** No. Longthaw, staged, stays. The cut scope that is now the default is Longthaw's own single-season minimum build, not a new concept; the day job strengthens its player-hosted model (no hosting bill, no on-call) over Ashward's developer-hosted valley; and the shared sim crate keeps Ashward's settlement-only slice as the further fallback if the ant farm is fun alone and the 30-minute run is not.

## 2. What changed in the inputs and what each does to the prior plan

**Rust education dropped.** The prior plan taxed year one 180-300 hours for the Rust ramp and kept Bevy partly so the Rust curriculum would live in sim, server, replication and persistence. Without the learning goal the ramp shrinks to an estimated 50-100 hours of review literacy (analyst, low; [Google ramp data](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2023/06/rust-fact-vs-fiction-5-insights-from-googles-rust-journey-2022.html)), and the client engine becomes a pure time-to-ship, maintainability and hiring question, which Bevy loses and which the gdext seam, on review, also loses (Section 5). Prior decision 8.3, "which code the founder writes by hand," becomes "which crates the founder reads line by line": sim, protocol/replication, persistence.

**Day job, 15-25 h/week.** The prior plan was sized at 2,025-2,250 logged hours a year. This report's first draft converted 20 h/week into ~770 productive hours a year (48 weeks, 0.8 productive) and moved every prior date by ~2.5x. The skeptic pass showed that arithmetic was itself a ceiling: 42 working weeks, 2-3 h/week of devlog and Thursday-group time and ~8% for engine and SDK upgrades leave ~385 build-hours a year at 15 h/week and ~540 at 20, so the prior dates move 3-5x, not 2.5x (Section 6). The Feb 2028 Next Fest and March-May 2028 EA do not survive at any tier below a funded studio. Gates convert from calendar weeks to cumulative-hour triggers, the review budget sets the pace (Section 7), and the pre-decided scope cut becomes the default scope rather than the fallback.

**Hiring asked about, no budget.** Hiring does not change the outcome distribution (Section 4) and costs 6-20% of founder capacity per contractor. It changes the engine choice at the margin (the first recurring hire is now a C# client contractor, one reason the client moves to C#) and what the first dollars buy: art by the piece and two one-off architecture reviews, then a client/feel contractor or community manager at the gate, a co-reviewer only when review saturates, never a programmer whose output the founder must read line by line before revenue.

**Unchanged.** Co-op from day one on an authoritative server; commercial viability drives design; no art team; Steam first, premium; the prior cash, price ladder, one-Next-Fest rule and base rates.

**Engagement shapes and plumbing.** Short milestone contracts (2-3 months, fixed deliverables, payment on acceptance) are what the market uses and postmortems favor; 18% of the game workforce is freelance ([JetBrains 2025](https://blog.jetbrains.com/dotnet/2026/01/29/game-dev-in-2025-excerpts-from-the-state-of-game-development-report/), medium) and 10-20 h/week retainers are common. Fixed-fee, time-boxed reviews (8-16 hours, written findings, no retainer) are the shape for senior input on architecture: they cost less than one month of any retainer and land where review leverage peaks. Refuse rev-share (over 90% of such projects never ship by community estimate, [itch.io](https://itch.io/blog/779232/rev-share-reality-check-why-splitting-profits-is-a-game-developers-nightmare), medium) and unpaid trials. Expect the first contractor relationship to fail: artists quitting inside a month and Upwork hires going silent recur in postmortems ([Crumbling World](https://danimarti.itch.io/crumbling-world/devlog/160435/crumbling-world-post-mortem-the-story-of-a-failure), medium). Before the first paid contractor: an agreement that "assigns all right, title and interest" (US work-for-hire alone does not cover contractors); for civil-law contractors an exclusive-usage-rights grant in writing, since in Germany [copyright cannot be assigned](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2023/09/Intellectual-Property-Assignments-from-Software-Developers) (medium); W-8BEN from non-US contractors working abroad (no 1099, no withholding); contractor-shaped control (deliverables, own hours, own tools) against the [Dutch DBA enforcement resumed 2025](https://www.deel.com/blog/dutch-zzp-dba-enforcement-false-self-employment-2026/) and UK IR35 tests (medium).

Three readings. First, the solo base-building cohort is the honest base case: 4-8 years to 1.0, $1-7M lifetime, HumanitZ-shaped ~$3M as the ceiling for Longthaw's shape. Lethal Company, Schedule I, R.E.P.O. and Peak are 2-6 month co-op-horror or management builds and belong outside the expected-value math. Second, every 4-7 person comp that reached $10M+ had a publisher and reached EA in 2-3 years; the self-published 5-person case is a $2M business. Third, headcount raises the floor cost faster than the hit rate: [Donkey Crew at ~30 was one miss from firing 90% of the studio](https://80.lv/articles/here-s-why-last-oasis-devs-started-a-new-project-before-completing-the-last-one/), while solo developers survived long misses; VG Insights puts 1-2 person teams at ~$50k typical revenue and 3-15 person teams at ~$1M ([medium](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-45776)). First non-founder hires in solo-origin teams were the founder's missing discipline (an artist for a programmer founder) or operations and QA once sales scaled; this is the pattern the revised first-hire recommendation follows.

**What a publisher substitutes for.** In every comp the publisher supplied runway, console ports, marketing beats, localization, fests and servers, never engineering: Coffee Stain funded five people to launch; Fireshine took Core Keeper to consoles; Modern Wolf supplied ZERO Sievert's trailer, Gamescom and Survival Fest ([medium](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/18/how-a-pixel-art-shooter-achieved-a-million-dollar-launch-the-zero-sievert-story/)); in the prior report's tag cohort, published titles reached 1,000 reviews 4/13 times against 1/34 self-published, selection unaccounted for. Terms range from [Hooded Horse's 65/35 lifetime split with no recoup and a $100k marketing floor](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-49908) to an industry median of ~50% developer share with an advance and ~70% without (medium). For a founder who cannot carry a team, the publisher is the first "hire"; it buys what agents cannot. Refuse 100%-recoup structures, which bite hardest at this lane's $1-3M median.

## 5. Engine and server re-decision without the learning goal

**Matrix, re-scored after review** (1-5 per criterion; analyst judgment on cited evidence, medium). Criteria in order: time-to-ship, assets and kits, animation and controller, Rust-server integration, sim-crate sharing, Steam Deck, consoles, hiring, agent effectiveness, compile-test loop, churn, licensing at $0-3M. The first draft scored Godot GDScript+gdext 49, Godot C# 48 and Unity 6 48 of 60, a tie broken by gdext's one-point edges on Rust-server integration and sim-crate sharing. Two corrections from the engine skeptic were adopted. Under the sidecar default the client carries no sim and needs only a socket, protocol decode and Steam calls, so those two criteria no longer separate the C# clients. And gdext's churn was scored 4 while it shipped breaking minors on 2025-09-29 (0.4.0) and 2026-03-27 (0.5.0) with a 28-heading migration guide for 0.5 (typed `Dictionary<K, V>`, `#[var]` get/set semantics, `GString::arg()` removed, module reorganization, `u64` banned in `#[func]`; [migration guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godot-rust/book/master/src/migrate/v0.5.md), verified), 0.6 pre-announced, and 29 of 34 recent commits by one maintainer ([skeptic's count](https://github.com/godot-rust/gdext/commits/master), high), so it is re-scored 2. The founder's three stated criteria (time-to-ship, churn as the maintainability proxy, hiring) are weighted 2x for the /75 column.

**Recommendation, revised.** The server half is unchanged: a Rust tokio authoritative server over SDR via steamworks-rs GameServer sockets ([v0.13.1](https://github.com/Noxime/steamworks-rs/releases), verified on crates.io; its README builds against Steamworks SDK 1.64), engine-free `sim`/`proto`/`net` crates, the sidecar listen server as the default, the one-day app-480 SDR spike before week 1. The client half changes in three moves. (1) gdext leaves the critical path. The client is C# with [Steamworks.NET](https://github.com/rlabrecque/Steamworks.NET) (README: SDK 1.65; ~3.6k stars per the skeptic, not re-fetched) or [Facepunch.Steamworks](https://github.com/Facepunch/Facepunch.Steamworks) (~3.8k stars) for Steam, and protocol decode in C#. To make that possible the wire format moves now from Rust-only postcard to a schema-driven cross-language format (protobuf via prost, or FlatBuffers if per-tick snapshot decode cost shows in the spike), with C# types generated in CI from the schema the Rust proto crate owns, so the proto crate stays the single source of truth; this costs days now and saves months later. Both Steam bindings and the redistributable are pinned to one SDK version in CI; today the Rust and C# bindings sit on 1.64 and 1.65, so the pin is a real task, not a default. (2) The client is an engine-agnostic .NET Standard 2.1 class library (protocol, Steam, interpolation buffers, typed events) that compiles unchanged into Godot 4.7 C# ([desktop platforms supported since 4.2; no web export](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot-docs/master/tutorials/scripting/c_sharp/index.rst), verified) or Unity 6, so the engine is a project swap rather than a rewrite, and a funded C# hire can work in either. (3) The engine is chosen by a bake-off, not by the matrix: agents build the identical vertical slice in both engines in parallel (third-person controller, one Synty kit, AnimationTree or Mecanim root motion, Steam overlay inside a Vulkan or URP build, sidecar connect through the shared library, Windows and Linux export), the founder logs review and play hours per engine, and the lower logged total wins. Pre-registered tie rule, written before the bake-off starts: within 15%, Unity 6, because the re-scored matrix leads on the founder's own criteria and the first recurring hire is now a C# client contractor from the pool the rate card prices widest and lowest; Godot 4.7 C# wins only by measured hours. The bake-off costs ~60-80 founder hours (agent time is a rounding error), so it runs over weeks 0-6 at 15-20 h/week, not weeks 0-2, and the winning slice becomes the client skeleton the prior plan costed at 160 hours. Skip Godot's MultiplayerAPI and Unity's NGO, Mirror and FishNet, which presuppose an engine peer on the server ([NGO has no full client-side prediction](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.11/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html), high). GodotSteam is no longer a fallback: every GitHub repo was archived on 2026-10-05 and moved to Codeberg, and its MultiplayerPeer flavor was last updated 2025-11-13 ([high](https://github.com/orgs/GodotSteam/repositories)).

**Engine maintenance, budgeted.** The first draft said "pin one 4.x line for the year"; that premise was wrong on maintenance. Godot ships a minor every ~5 months (4.6 January 2026, 4.7 June 2026, 4.8 estimated Q4 2026) and its written policy supports a stable branch "at least until the next stable branch is released and has received its first patch update", best effort after that, with "minor compatibility breakage in very specific areas" allowed per minor ([release policy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot-docs/master/about/release_policy.rst), verified). A 2031-33 Early Access therefore passes through 8-14 Godot minors or ships on a best-effort build. Plan two minor upgrades a year at 8-20 hours each and never ship a build on a minor that has lost guaranteed patch support. Unity: pin to the LTS stream and accept Pro at ~$2,200 per seat per year above $200k revenue plus funding ([medium](https://unity.com/pricing-updates), not re-verified). Either way, upgrades, SDK bumps and toolchain take ~8% of build hours (Section 6). If gdext is ever wanted as a measured accelerator it carries a written C# fallback and a tracked bus-factor risk; GDExtension forward compatibility (an extension built on 4.6 runs on 4.7+) is confirmed ([godot-rust book](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godot-rust/book/master/src/toolchain/compatibility.md), verified) but covers only the Rust binary, not the project, editor or export templates.

Why over Bevy: 0.20 is rc.2 today with its release post a hidden draft ([crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/bevy), verified); 0.17 shipped 127 migration guides and 0.18 89, so a 2031-33 EA on Bevy means 4-7 upgrades at 40-120 h each; no editor, 3D character tooling or store-kit support; Rust is where coding agents scored lowest on repository-level benchmarks in early 2025 ([Multi-SWE-bench: GPT-4o Python 36.2% vs Rust 5.9%](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/multi-swe-bench/multi-swe-bench/main/README.md), low; the README fetched 2026-10-07 no longer carries the per-language table, so the figure is unverified); and the hire this founder actually needs, art and tech art, has contractor pools for Unity and Godot and none for Bevy. Why not simply Unity: NGO, Mirror and FishNet add nothing when the server is Rust, so a Unity client writes the same thin protocol as a Godot one; Personal caps at $200k revenue plus funding, then $2,200 per seat per year; the 2023 runtime-fee episode is a trust cost the matrix scores under licensing; and Godot's zero licence and native Linux and Deck story are real. Unity wins hiring (135k Discord, first-party consoles, best animation tooling) and the asset and controller columns, which are the founder's weak areas. None of that is decisive, which is why logged hours decide and the matrix is only the tie rule. Unreal is out: C++ loop with header-change restarts, server targets tied to UE replication, Rust interop a hobby proof of concept, 5% over $1M.

**Server language.** Rust stays; it is the one part of the stack that should not move. It is the founder's strength; agents are strongest on backend code with a test oracle ([Anthropic's 16-agent run produced a 100k-line C compiler passing 99% of the GCC torture suite for under $20k](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler), high); backend-Rust contractors are plentiful at ~$50-55/hr; and determinism testing lets one reviewer verify agent output at scale (Section 7). A solo Rust authoritative-server game exists in public at ~9 merged commits a day, 61.5% Claude co-authored ([OpenMMO](https://github.com/Julian-adv/OpenMMO), high); its client is Svelte, TypeScript and Three.js, so it is precedent for the server half only, not for a Godot or Unity client.

**The biggest risks, revised.** (1) The first draft's top risk, the Rust-in-Godot seam (gdext's commercially released list is two casual titles, Solitaire Klondike Classic on mobile and Graphwar II on Steam and mobile, neither 3D nor co-op, [games page](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godot-rust/book/master/src/ecosystem/games.md), verified; Godot's own 66-title showcase has no 3D online co-op survival game, [high](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-website/tree/master/collections/_showcase)), is retired by taking gdext off the critical path. Its replacement is the schema seam: generated C# types must stay in lockstep with the Rust proto crate, which is a CI check (regenerate, compile, protocol-version bump), not prose. (2) Consoles: Rust has [no PS5 or Xbox targets](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rust-lang/rust/master/src/doc/rustc/src/platform-support.md) (high) and W4 Consoles' GDExtension support is unverified; with a C# client and a server that ships only on PC, the console problem narrows to a Rust-free sim for console single-player, post-1.0. (3) New: the sidecar's lifecycle. `OS.create_process` "creates a new process that runs independently of Godot. It will not terminate when Godot terminates" ([OS.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/godotengine/godot/master/doc/classes/OS.xml), verified), and .NET's `Process.Start` behaves the same, so a client crash orphans a listen server holding the save. The watchdog is a week-0 item: the server exits when its control socket goes silent for N seconds, the client records the PID and kills it on clean exit, and saves are written at tick boundaries so an orphan can lose at most one tick. (4) Steam overlay inside a Godot Vulkan build is a spike item, not a given ([issues #73532, #74772, #84251](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues?q=is%3Aissue+steam+overlay+vulkan), medium).

**Is "Rust sim core in Godot via gdext plus headless Rust server" sound, or clever-but-risky?** Revised answer: the skeptic review settled it. The server half and the shared-crate half are sound: engine-free crates, a tokio binary, the founder's home ground. The client half was clever-but-risky twice over: running the sim on a thread inside the engine process for solo and listen play, and the gdext seam itself, which the sidecar default made unnecessary. Both are removed. The in-process listen server is no longer on the roadmap even as an optimization; solo play is the same sidecar on localhost. Weeks 0-6 spikes, revised: the schema pipeline (Rust proto crate to generated C# types in CI); the shared .NET library connecting to the sidecar, with the watchdog; the bake-off slice in both engines with logged hours; Steam overlay inside a Vulkan (Godot) and URP (Unity) build through the C# binding; Windows and Linux (Deck) exports; the one-day SDR GameServer spike on app 480; the first fixed-fee senior Rust architecture review at the end, covering the deterministic-sim harness, wire protocol and versioning, persistence and save migration, and the sidecar contract.

## 6. The part-time plan

**The 3,000-hour sanity check, stated plainly.** 3,000 hours over 48 weeks is 62.5 hours a week. On top of a 40-45 hour job that is ~105 hours a week, leaving ~63, about nine a day, for sleep, meals, commuting and family. It sits permanently in the band where [WHO/ILO found +35% stroke and +17% ischemic heart disease risk](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2021-long-working-hours-increasing-deaths-from-heart-disease-and-stroke-who-ilo) (55+ h/week vs 35-40, high) and beyond the ~50 hours where [Pencavel](https://docs.iza.org/dp8129.pdf) found output per hour falling (medium, supporting). No comp sustained it; Barone's ~70 h/week on Stardew with a part-time usher job ended in burnout ([medium](https://www.pcgamer.com/stardew-valley-interview/)). The figure stays withdrawn.

**The 15-25 estimate against what day-job developers sustain.** Developers with full-time jobs report 4-12 h/week over multi-year spans ([gamedev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/650239-hours-per-week), medium-low), and dual jobholders add ~8-10 h/week to a main job ([Statistics Canada, 45.9 vs 36.1 h/week](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/75-001-x/1998002/article/3825-eng.pdf), medium). The founder's estimate is 2-4x that norm, so 20 h/week is the ceiling of the base case and 10-15 the likely drift after month six. The first draft stated that and then computed every date at 20; the timeline skeptic caught the contradiction. Revised: the base case is 15 h/week, the floor of the founder's estimate and the ceiling of what day-job developers sustain; 20 is an upside the founder must demonstrate with logged hours; and a 55-hour total cap on top of a 40-45 hour job leaves exactly 10-15 project hours, which is why 20 is sprint-only unless the job shrinks. No day-job comp took an online co-op game to Early Access: Vampire Survivors and Bright Memory shipped tiny single-player games in ~12-14 months; Balatro and Valheim used the day-job year for a prototype, then went full-time (Valheim with four more people and a publisher, [medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valheim)); Kenshi and Dinkum took 12 and 5-8 years.

## 7. Agents as the team

**Review-bandwidth math.** Careful review tops out around 200-400 changed lines per 60-90 minute session; above ~450 lines an hour defect density is below average in 87% of cases ([Cisco/SmartBear, 2,500 reviews](https://github.com/vasilyu1983/AI-Agents-public/blob/main/skills/universal/software-code-review/references/large-pr-review-strategies.md), medium, via mirror); Google's norm is ~100-line changes and one-business-day turnaround ([high](https://github.com/google/eng-practices/blob/master/review/developer/small-cls.md)). At 40% of hours in review and ~250 lines an hour: 10-15 h/week reviews ~1,000-1,500 changed lines (5-10 PRs of 200 lines or fewer); 15-25 h/week ~1,500-2,500 (8-15 PRs); 25-35 h/week ~2,500-3,500 (15-25 PRs). Agents generate far more ([29,000 lines in 10 days with 8 parallel agents](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-03-22-solo-devs-shipping-games-with-ai), low-medium), so the pipeline is throttled to the review budget with a WIP limit. Scale check added after review: ~2,000 reviewed lines a week is ~84k a year over 42 weeks and ~420k across the five years to a P50 Early Access; OpenMMO's single maintainer passed a net ~323k lines (176k Rust, 92k TypeScript, 55k Svelte) in about eight active months at ~314 commits a month ([skeptic's clone of the repo](https://github.com/Julian-adv/OpenMMO/commits/master), high), so the entire runway buys roughly one OpenMMO of review, and the slow solo Rust-server comparable, [parabellum](https://github.com/andreapavoni/parabellum) (65k Rust lines, one author, 22 months, "not complete or full-featured"), is the shape a 10-15 h/week project actually takes. Review volume, not agent output, is the project's clock. Do not plan a founder multiplier above ~1.3x: the only RCT found experienced developers [19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster](https://github.com/METR/Measuring-Early-2025-AI-on-Exp-OSS-Devs) (high), and more than half of Anthropic's own engineers can fully delegate only [0-20% of their work](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic) (high). Agent cost is a rounding error: $200-600/month, rising toward ~$1k with the evaluator in the gate.

**Where agents are strong and weak here.** Strong: backend and systems code with a test oracle, as in the compiler run, whose failure mode was regression ("frequently broke existing functionality"). Weak: game feel and art-adjacent work. A solo agent run produced a Game Maker app with [non-functional gameplay for $9; only a separate evaluator that played the running build got working gameplay, at 22x the cost](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps) (high); models "confidently praise work even when obviously mediocre"; one frontier model failed rigging outright ([OpenMMO devlog](https://github.com/Julian-adv/Julian-adv.github.io/blob/main/src/content/blog/modular-characters.md), medium). The agent failure mode for games is playability, not server logic, which is the second reason the first recurring contractor is a client/feel engineer and not a backend reviewer. Review itself degrades: reviewers habituated to AI PRs within months (approval 30.1% to 36.8%, inline comments -22%, [low-medium, via mirror](https://github.com/testdouble/han/blob/main/docs/research/collaborative-output-style.md)), and Cloudflare's security-engineer-reviewed, Claude-written OAuth library still shipped [two Moderate advisories](https://github.com/cloudflare/workers-oauth-provider/security/advisories) (high). DORA finds AI adoption [degrades delivery stability](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report) only "without robust control systems" (high), that is, the fix is gates, not extra eyes. On an authoritative server unreviewed code is a cheat and data-loss surface.

**Structure.** Line-by-line founder review in three crates: sim, proto/replication, persistence. Evidence review (tests, replay seed, screenshot or proof recording) for client glue, tooling, CI, localization and the County service. The sim crate is deterministic-sim-testable from week 1 on the TigerBeetle pattern: clock, randomness and I/O stubbed, a seeded PRNG at the boundary, thousands of seeded sessions nightly, seed plus commit printed on any invariant failure ([VOPR](https://github.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle/blob/main/docs/internals/vopr.md), high); proptest conservation laws; an architecture test that fails CI if any sim file imports engine types, `Instant`, `thread_rng` or does I/O, as [World of ClaudeCraft](https://github.com/levy-street/world-of-claudecraft/blob/main/docs/qa-gate.md) enforces across 19,885 commits (high). Every rule is a hook or CI gate, not prose: a Stop hook runs fmt, clippy, tests for touched crates, a protocol-version check and C# type regeneration plus compile when the wire schema changes, and a save-migration test when serde structs change; editing or deleting a test needs founder approval; both Steam bindings and the redistributable pinned to one SDK version workspace-wide; the pinned engine (Godot 4.7 or Unity 6 LTS), its upgrade notes and the Steam binding versions in a version-pinned skill. Specialist agents with ownership: a spec agent writing SPEC.md with testable acceptance criteria; writers for sim, net, persist, client (C#, pinned engine, screenshot-verified) and tools (CI, release, localization, County); a reviewer in a fresh context seeing only diff plus spec, flagging correctness gaps only; an evaluator that launches the headless server plus bot clients, plays the criteria and records proof, and which sits in the merge gate before any human reviewer is paid (the harness result shows this is what turns agent-built games from non-functional to playable, at ~20x solo-agent cost and still under ~$1k/month); a release agent that prepares builds while "releases are for humans only" ([Anthropic best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices), high). Gate order: spec, hooks, reviewer, evaluator proof, founder review within budget, merge queue plus nightly, human-only Steam upload; CLAUDE.md under 200 lines. Nightly 2-4 player bot sessions hash sim state per tick and watch for guest-inventory loss, the lane's named co-op failure mode. Game feel lives in data tables the founder edits with the Thursday group; rigging and animation are commissioned. Every merged change is one the founder can explain without the agent ([Ghostty](https://github.com/ghostty-org/ghostty/blob/main/AI_POLICY.md), high).

**The hire question, re-answered after review.** The first draft named a fractional senior Rust co-reviewer at 4-8 h/week as "the only hire that acts on the binding constraint." The first-hire skeptic refuted it on structure: the Ghostty rule above keeps the founder on the critical path of every sim, proto and persist merge, so a second reviewer buys assurance, not throughput, while charging 1.5-4 h/week of coordination (6-20% of the week); net founder review capacity can fall. The evidence on what outside reviewers contribute agrees: at Microsoft only ~15% of review comments indicate a possible defect and half or more are maintainability remarks, and a reviewer new to the code base is useful 33% of the time, 67% by the third review ([Czerwonka, Greiler and Tilford, ICSE 2015](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/PID3556473.pdf); [Bosu, Greiler and Bird, MSR 2015](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/characteristics-of-useful-code-reviews-an-empirical-study-at-microsoft/), high); Rigby and Bird found two reviewers find an optimal number of defects and that reviewer expertise, not process, explains most defect variance ([high](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/rigby2013convergent.pdf)); with the deterministic harness catching functional regressions, the marginal catch of a 4-6 h/week generalist is small, and even Cloudflare's senior reviewer missed two advisories. Where senior Rust input does pay is design, at the moments determinism, wire protocol, persistence and the sidecar contract are set: hence the two fixed-fee architecture reviews ($500-1,500 each) at the end of the weeks 0-6 spikes and at the two-player loop, with a review swap with another indie Rust developer as the $0 version. The recurring co-reviewer survives only under two conditions, both new: a **review-saturation trigger**, independent of wishlists (for eight consecutive weeks the sim/proto/persist merge queue holds more than two weeks of founder-reviewable backlog at the 8-15 PR/week cap, or nightly seeded runs produce more invariant failures than the founder can triage inside the 40% review budget), and a written change to the merge rule delegating one crate's line-by-line review (net/replication is the candidate) to the co-reviewer, without which the merge rate cannot move. Recruit from the backend-Rust community, not games, where 59% of programmers view gen-AI negatively ([GDC 2026, via mirror](https://github.com/lucasbrandao4770/claude-code-gamedev/blob/main/notes/research/ai-gamedev-community-2026.md), medium-low).

## 8. Does the concept change?

No. Longthaw, staged, stays the default over Ashward, for three reasons. First, the day job favors the player-hosted model: Longthaw's listen server plus free dedicated binary has no hosting bill, no population floor and no on-call, while Ashward's developer-hosted persistent valley puts a 24/7 service on a founder who is at a day job when it falls over. Second, the commercial case is unchanged: Longthaw led commercial viability 6.5 to 5 in the prior re-score and Ashward's only solo comp (Ymir) did not improve. Third, the shared simulation crate still makes Ashward's settlement-only slice the further fallback: if Gate 0 (the ant farm is fun alone) passes and Gate 1 (a 30-minute run is fun alone) fails, the expedition half is cut on the same crate.

What changes after review is the default scope and the shape of the first phase. The cut scope is now what ships: Longthaw single season, the hearth with three settler jobs, one kit, one region, one enemy family, 2-player listen server only, with the SDR dedicated binary, 4-player drop-in, the Meltline, the second kit and the eight-language pass as post-EA updates earned by demonstrated hours. That is the prior report's own minimum shippable build, so it is still Longthaw, not a new concept. The prior year-one deliverable (demo, Next Fest slot, wishlist pile) becomes a 20-28 month loop-and-page phase (loop Feb-Mar 2028 and page May-Jun 2028 at a demonstrated 20 h/week; Aug-Sep 2028 and Jan-Feb 2029 at the 15 h/week base) whose output is the evidence for the 90-day gate's three-way choice: a four-day week or sabbatical funded from savings, a publisher advance, or hobby pace on the cut scope with Early Access in 2031-33 accepted in writing. The third-person camera stays the default on the catalog base rate (self-published 3D survival co-op 17.6% against 2.9% top-down at 1,000 reviews, high from the dump), but its estimated 100-200 extra founder hours are now 13-26% of the base-case 24-month build budget at 15 h/week (9-19% at 20), which makes the camera, controller and animation pass the first deliverable of the client/feel contractor at the gate, and the item Unity's Starter Assets and Mecanim or Godot's AnimationTree root motion and Synty's packs most directly cheapen. A 2031-33 EA also meets a market the prior report did not score: Zomboid B42 and HumanitZ 1.0 will be five to seven years older and new entrants will have taken the cheap differentiators, which argues for keeping the calendar hook on the capsule and spending the first dollars on it, not for a new concept. No other prior concept improves for a part-time, zero-hire, review-bound founder.

## 9. Decisions the founder must make now

1. **Accept the bands in writing, or change the inputs.** Default: accept, zero hires, cut scope, 15 h/week base: Early Access mid-2031 (P20), mid-2033 (P50), late 2035 (P90); loop Aug-Sep 2028, page Jan-Feb 2029. Feb 2028 dropped at every tier below a funded studio; Oct 2028 only at tier C with a one-in-four chance; Oct 2029 is the sprint-band stretch. The inputs that move the bands are hours (decision 10), employment (decision 14) and money (Section 3), in that order.
2. **Client engine.** Default, revised: a C# client on an engine-agnostic .NET Standard 2.1 library; Godot 4.7 C# versus Unity 6 decided by the weeks 0-6 bake-off on logged founder hours for the identical vertical slice, with Unity 6 as the pre-registered tie rule (within 15%); gdext off the critical path, kept only as an optional measured accelerator with a written C# fallback; the wire format moved from postcard to a schema-driven cross-language format (protobuf via prost, or FlatBuffers) chosen in the spike; two engine minor upgrades a year budgeted at 8-20 hours each, never shipping on a minor that has lost guaranteed patch support.
3. **Listen server: sidecar or in-process.** Default: sidecar via `OS.create_process` or `Process.Start`, with the watchdog (server exits on a silent control socket, client kills the recorded PID on clean exit, saves at tick boundaries), because the child "will not terminate when Godot terminates"; the in-process listen server is removed from the roadmap.
4. **Server stack.** Default: unchanged Rust tokio binary, steamworks-rs GameServer over SDR, one-day spike on app 480 before week 1, raw-UDP fallback; plus one Steamworks SDK version pinned across steamworks-rs, the C# binding and the redistributable in CI (today 1.64 and 1.65).
5. **Hours.** Default, revised: total cap 55 a week including the job (not ~60), logged weekly; project 15 h/week as the base; 20+ only inside 8-12 week sprints before the page, demo and fest, each followed by a deload fortnight; plan on 42 project weeks a year.
6. **Review budget and merge policy.** Default: 8-15 PRs/week at 200 changed lines or fewer, hard cap 400; line-by-line in sim, proto and persist, evidence elsewhere; every rule a hook; the evaluator agent in the merge gate before any human reviewer is paid; a WIP limit that keeps the queue inside the budget; releases human-only.
7. **First spend.** Default: yes, from petty cash, $300-1,500/month before the page: capsule (~$400) plus $500-1,000 of capsule and art-direction iteration pulled into the page month, settler rig, one kit, per-participant playtests; two fixed-fee senior Rust architecture reviews ($500-1,500 each) at the end of the weeks 0-6 spikes and at the two-player loop, or a review swap at $0.
8. **First hire and its trigger.** Default, revised: none until the 90-day post-page gate (Apr-May 2029 at 15 h/week, Aug-Sep 2028 at a demonstrated 20); at ~1,500-2,000 organic wishlists or a live publisher talk, a client/gameplay-and-feel engineer or technical artist in the chosen engine's C# pool at 10-15 h/week on a 3-month milestone contract, evidence-reviewed; a part-time community manager if wishlists bind; a fractional senior Rust co-reviewer only on the review-saturation trigger of Section 7 and only with one crate's line-by-line review delegated in writing. Never a second output stream the founder must read line by line before revenue.
9. **Publisher.** Default: open conversations at the 90-day gate if velocity is strong; benchmark against Hooded Horse's 65/35 no-recoup terms; refuse 100%-recoup; a publisher advance is one of the three gate options in decision 14.
10. **Scope: cut is the default, add-back is earned.** Default, revised: the cut scope (single season, one kit, one region, 2-player listen only) is the shipped scope from day one; anything cut is restored only after 18+ h/week logged for six consecutive months on rolling 4-week windows; a standing quarterly check cuts further (to Ashward's settlement-only slice) after two consecutive quarters averaging under 12 project hours a week or on self-reported fatigue, replacing the first draft's one 8-week window after month six.
11. **Camera.** Default: third-person stays, locked by a prototype of both cameras at ~400 hours (Oct-Nov 2027 at 15 h/week, Jun-Jul 2027 at 20); first deliverable for the client/feel contractor at the gate.
12. **Legal plumbing before any contractor.** Default: assignment-of-rights template with a civil-law variant, W-8BEN collection, contractor-shaped control.
13. **Next Fest discipline.** Default, revised: never register under ~2,000 pre-fest wishlists or before the demo has been public a month; register and open publisher talks against the P50 demo date at logged hours (Feb 2031 at a demonstrated 20 h/week, Oct 2032 at the 15 h/week base), with the P20 date (Feb 2030 / 2031) as the stretch, never the plan.
14. **Employment decision at the 90-day gate (new).** Default: write the three options now and choose one at the gate on the evidence it produces: a four-day week or sabbatical funded from a named savings runway, which is what the comps that shipped online co-op after a day-job prototype year actually did; a publisher advance (decision 9); or hobby pace on the cut scope with Early Access in 2031-33 accepted in writing. Not deciding is the fourth option and is the one the first draft silently assumed.

### Skeptic review (2026-10-07)

Three skeptics attacked the first draft; the editor adopted each mitigation in full or in part as listed. Where the skeptics disagreed with each other (the timeline skeptic's mitigation kept the co-reviewer at the page gate; the first-hire skeptic refuted it), the conflict was resolved in favor of the first-hire verdict, because the timeline skeptic's own structural point, that the merge rate is bounded by founder reading hours, is the same argument.

1. **Claim: the first recurring hire should be a fractional senior Rust co-reviewer because it is the only hire that multiplies review throughput.** Verdict: refuted (medium). Strongest argument: the co-reviewer acts on the wrong side of the founder's own merge rule (every sim/proto/persist line founder-read, "explain every merged change without the agent"), so it cannot raise the merge rate and its 1.5-4 h/week of coordination can lower net founder capacity; review studies put defect-finding at ~15% of review comments and show expertise, not extra eyes, drives defect yield; the report's own Tier B row gave a client/feel contractor, reviewed by evidence and so bypassing the bottleneck, a larger multiplier where the founder is weakest; the wishlist gate is a demand signal, not a review-saturation signal; and senior Rust input peaks at design time (weeks 0-6, the loop), not as a retainer after the architecture is frozen. What changed: Sections 1, 2, 3, 7, 8 and decisions 7, 8. The first recurring contractor is a client/gameplay-and-feel engineer or technical artist; senior Rust input becomes two fixed-fee architecture reviews ($500-1,500 each); the co-reviewer is decoupled from wishlists behind a review-saturation trigger and a written delegation of one crate's line-by-line review; the evaluator agent enters the merge gate before any paid human review; $500-1,000 of capsule and art-direction iteration is pulled into the page month. Caveat kept from the skeptic: reviewers ramp fast (67% useful by the third review), so lack of context was not the reason; structure and trigger were. The programmer-founder-hires-artist comps (Factorio, RimWorld, Valheim) could not be verified and are not relied on.
2. **Claim: Godot with typed GDScript and a thin Rust GDExtension is the fastest credible client path with acceptable risk.** Verdict: refuted on the client half, upheld on the server half (medium). Strongest argument: under the report's own sidecar default the client carries no sim and needs only socket, protocol decode and Steam calls, so gdext's one-point matrix edge is worthless, while gdext shipped breaking minors on 2025-09-29 and 2026-03-27 (verified), has a bus factor near one, lists two casual commercially released titles and no 3D or co-op game, and the "pin one 4.x line for the year" premise ignores Godot's ~5-month minor cadence and support window (verified); the named fallbacks were degraded (GodotSteam archived 2026-10-05) and `OS.create_process` does not kill its child on exit (verified); mature C# Steam bindings exist at roughly eight times the adoption of the Rust path; and the matrix's own columns for the founder's three criteria favor Unity 6. What changed: Sections 1, 2, 5, 7 and decisions 2, 3, 4. The server half is unchanged. The client becomes C# on an engine-agnostic .NET Standard 2.1 library; the wire format moves from postcard to a schema-driven format with generated C# types; the engine is decided by a weeks 0-6 bake-off on logged hours with Unity 6 as the pre-registered tie rule; the matrix was re-scored (Unity 6 62, Godot C# 58, GDScript+gdext 55 of 75 weighted); engine maintenance is budgeted at two minors a year; the sidecar gets a watchdog; the Multi-SWE-bench Rust figure is flagged unverified; OpenMMO is noted as server-side precedent only. Not adopted: the skeptic's option of keeping gdext as a measured accelerator remains allowed but is not planned.
3. **Claim: at 20 h/week the plan reaches a public demo in April 2029, the Next Fest in October 2029 and Early Access by March 2030 without burnout.** Verdict: refuted (medium-high). Strongest argument: the dates required three stacked assumptions the report's own evidence put below even odds: 20 h/week for 42 straight months when the report itself said 10-15 is the likely drift after month six; "likely" hour triggers carried over unchanged from a prior plan that had already run 1.4x over, with no pessimistic band printed; and zero budgeted hours for engine upgrades, devlogs, the Thursday group, orchestration and regression fixing, on top of 48 working weeks. The health claim failed too: 20 h on a 40-45 h job is 60-65 h total, inside the WHO/ILO 55+ band and at the report's own ~60 cap for 3.5 years. The whole runway bought roughly one OpenMMO of founder review. What changed: Sections 1, 2, 3, 6, 8 and decisions 1, 5, 10, 13, 14. Build-hours re-derived at 42 weeks, 2.5 h/week community, 8% upgrades (~385/yr at 15 h/week, ~540 at 20); base case 15 h/week on the cut scope with 20 as a demonstrated upside; P20/P50/P90 bands at x1.0/x1.4/x1.9 likely hours; fest registration and publisher talks against the P50 date; total-hours cap 55; standing quarterly scope check; employment decision written into the 90-day gate. Partially adopted: the skeptic's 10-15% upgrade line was reduced to 8% because gdext is retired; the base case was set at 15 rather than 12-15 because the founder's own estimate starts at 15 and the 55-hour cap on a 40-hour job lands there. Note on the 1.4x: in the prior report it is the ratio of likely hours to hours available, not a measured estimation error; it is used here as a judgment that the "likely" point is a P20, supported by the comps and METR.

Editor's check after integration: every date in the Section 6 table and the gate list was re-derived from the stated build-hour formula (logged hours minus 2.5, x 42 weeks, x 0.8, x 0.92) and the likely-hour triggers; all reproduce within one month. Two ranges that did not (the "3.5-5x" slip of the prior dates in Section 2, now 3-5x; the hobby-pace years in Section 6, now 7-11 staged and 5-8 cut) were corrected. The timeline skeptic's comps for the multiplayer half (Zomboid Build 42, Sapiens, Survivalist) and for burnout (Bithell, GDC 2026) were cited only in the prior report and are now cited in Section 6 at the prior report's confidence.
